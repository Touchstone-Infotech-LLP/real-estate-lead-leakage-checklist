## Why This Checklist Exists

Real estate businesses often evaluate marketing primarily through lead volume and cost per lead.

But a low cost per lead does not necessarily mean successful marketing.

If enquiries are not contacted quickly, qualified properly, matched with the right project and followed up consistently, increasing advertising spend may simply increase lead leakage.

This assessment helps you examine what happens **after the lead arrives**.

---

## What You'll Assess

The checklist evaluates seven important areas of the real estate lead-to-revenue journey.

### 1. Marketing Attribution

Can you identify where every lead came from and which campaign, ad or keyword generated it?

### 2. Lead Quality

Can you distinguish between total leads, contactable leads, relevant prospects and genuinely qualified buyers?

### 3. First Response & Sales Accountability

Can you see who received each lead, when the first contact attempt happened and what happened afterwards?

### 4. Lead Qualification & Project Matching

Are buyers being qualified based on budget, property requirement, location and buying timeline?

And when a buyer does not fit one project, are they checked against other suitable inventory?

### 5. Follow-Up & Lead Recovery

Do qualified prospects have a clear next follow-up action, or are opportunities being lost because follow-up depends on individual salespeople?

### 6. Site Visit Management

Can you track site visits through stages such as:

**Scheduled → Confirmed → Completed → No-show → Rescheduled → Negotiation → Booking**

### 7. Management & Revenue Visibility

Can management connect marketing sources with qualified leads, site visits and bookings without manually combining multiple reports?

# Why Cost Per Lead Alone Can Be Misleading

Consider two campaigns:

**Campaign A:** ₹600 cost per lead  
**Campaign B:** ₹1,000 cost per lead

At first glance, Campaign A looks better.

But what if Campaign B produces substantially more qualified buyers and completed site visits?

The more useful question is not simply:

**“Which campaign generated cheaper leads?”**

It is:

**“Which campaign generated better business outcomes?”**

---

## Four Metrics Worth Tracking

### Cost Per Lead

**Marketing Spend ÷ Total Leads**

### Cost Per Qualified Lead

**Marketing Spend ÷ Qualified Leads**

### Cost Per Completed Site Visit

**Marketing Spend ÷ Completed Site Visits**

### Cost Per Booking

**Marketing Spend ÷ Bookings**

# The 5-Minute Reality Test

Pick one booking or completed site visit from the last 30 days.

Try tracing it backwards:

**Booking → Negotiation / Revisit → Completed Site Visit → Qualification → Sales Follow-Up → First Contact → Original Enquiry → Lead Source → Campaign / Ad**

If you can trace the entire journey within a few minutes, your tracking infrastructure is probably in good shape.

If the trail disappears somewhere, that is one of the first areas worth investigating.
